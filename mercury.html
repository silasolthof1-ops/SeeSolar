Mercury

Mercury is the smallest planet and the closest to the Sun. Its ancient, cratered surface resembles Earth's Moon, while the near-vacuum around it allows temperatures to swing dramatically between day and night.

An intense world

A solar day on Mercury lasts 176 Earth days, even though the planet circles the Sun in just 88. Its surface can reach about 430°C (800°F) in daylight and fall to around −180°C (−290°F) at night.

Mercury has a large iron-rich core, a non-existent atmosphere, and a weak magnetic field. This lack of atmospheric and magnetic field protection means large, frequent meteorite impacts and extremely large temperature swings.

Mercury is not the hottest planet. Its thin exosphere cannot hold heat, so Venus has the higher average surface temperature.
