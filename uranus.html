Uranus

Uranus is a distant ice giant with a blue-green atmosphere. Its extreme axial tilt makes it appear to roll around the Sun on its side, producing unusually long seasons.

A tipped-over world

Uranus is mostly made of hot, dense fluids rich in water, methane, and ammonia above a small rocky core. Methane in the upper atmosphere absorbs red light, giving the planet its blue-green color.

The planet has faint rings and a complex family of moons. Voyager 2 flew past Uranus in 1986; it remains the only spacecraft to have visited this faraway world.

Because Uranus is tipped by about 98 degrees, each pole experiences roughly 21 years of continuous sunlight followed by 21 years of darkness.
